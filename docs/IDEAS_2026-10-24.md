### 4. Bots that fill empty lobbies (your Feature A)

  - "Use PathfindingService with state machines." **Conflict with CLAUDE.md rule 5** (no per-frame PathfindingService).

### 5. Spectator mode (your Feature B)

### 8. Frame-stepped movement for a nostalgic feel, with the game still at 55 to 60 fps
- **Today:** `StepAnimationController` already makes every character's **animation** change pose 8 times a second
  (characters farther than 120 studs animate normally), while the game itself runs at full frame rate.
- **What "all character movement" could mean:** (a) the animation steps we have, extended to your own first-person body and
  arms; (b) other characters' **positions** also snapping at the step rate; (c) your own movement stepping. I would do
  (a), consider (b), and avoid (c): stepping your own movement adds input lag and would hurt aiming. See Q1.
- **Frame rate:** pose stepping costs the same or less than smooth animation, so 55 to 60 fps is safe. (The crowd's
  client cost is about 2.5 ms a frame today.)

### 9. See your own body in first person (looking down shows the whole body)

- **Plan:** show your torso, legs and feet; hide only the **head and its accessories** (hair, hat, sunglasses), so the camera

- **Tied to the gun:** instead of separate sleeve blocks, the view model uses your **real arms**, posed by animation to hold

- **Constraint:** nothing may be put on a character that marks it as a player (rule 3). All of it is client-side

### 10. The gun must stay in view
- **Likely cause (I can see it in the numbers):** at the default 70 degree field of view, a gun 1.25 studs ahead of the camera
  is only visible down to about 0.88 studs below the sight line. The disguised "Tucked" pose sits **1.35 studs below**, which
  is off the bottom of the screen. The pose was placed without checking the view frustum.
- **Fix:** place every pose from the **actual field of view and aspect ratio**, and clamp the sway so it can never carry the
  gun off the edge, at any aspect ratio and while zoomed. I will confirm with screenshots in Studio.

### 11. The gun must never pass through a wall
- **Plan:** every frame, cast rays (centre and both sides, up and down) forward from the camera along the gun's length. If
  something is closer than the gun is long, **pull the gun back and tuck it** (raise the muzzle to a high-ready pose, or
  lower it for a low-ready) in proportion to how close the wall is. Blends smoothly both ways, and shooting is blocked
  while it is fully tucked (you cannot fire through a wall you are pressed against).
- This is the "collision box and tuck animation" from your note, done with rays because it is cheaper and more exact than a
  physical collision box on a client-only model.

### 12. Slimmer and slightly taller characters
- **Today:** every outfit scales 95% to 105% in height, width and depth, for players and NPCs alike (spec 8.2: the
  same ranges, so size is never a tell).
- **Plan:** move the **whole range** for everyone: taller, and noticeably slimmer in width and depth, keeping the same
  spread so size still tells nothing. Things that must follow: hit-box radius, the camera height, door and vent sizes
  (check they still fit and feel right), the NPC pathing height, the clothes (so slim bodies do not look odd), and the
  first-person view. See Q8 for the numbers.

### 13. Floatier movement (not too much)
- **Two kinds of "floaty":**
  - **In the air:** a bit less gravity and a slightly longer jump. Jumping is already something Security notices, so this
    leaks nothing.
  - **On the ground:** speeding up and slowing down smoothly instead of instantly. **This is an identity risk:** NPCs
    start and stop instantly today, so players who ease in and out would be a tell (spec 8.1 lists movement tells as
    intentional only for sprint, jump, crouch and climb). Safe options: give NPCs the same ease-in and ease-out, or keep the
    floatiness to the air only. See Q9.
- **Knock-ons:** fall damage already uses the real gravity, so it stays correct; ziplines and the gondola set their own speed.
